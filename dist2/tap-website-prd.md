## Part 1 — Strategy

### 1. Product mandate

The website must make The Athletes Prayer Foundation feel credible enough to enter a professional locker room and accountable enough to receive a major gift.

TAP is a lean, Atlanta-based 501(c)(3) nonprofit social enterprise led by Founder and Executive Director Michael Drew. It serves professional, collegiate, and high school athletes through:

1. Faith-centered prayer support.
2. TAP-credentialed spiritual mentorship.
3. The TAP Time Capsule—a personalized prayer scroll delivered as a physical, career-long archive.
4. Team and school partnerships.
5. Philanthropic support that expands access to athletes and programs.

The primary brand promise is:

> We provide a permanent spiritual anchor through tangible prayer archives that ensure your identity outlasts your jersey.

The website must support two materially different actions:

- **Enroll or purchase:** Pay for a defined TAP program or product.
- **Give or partner:** Make a charitable contribution to advance TAP’s mission.

These actions may support the same mission, but they must never appear financially or semantically interchangeable.

### 2. Mission and 501(c)(3) positioning

#### Mission

Use TAP’s current mission language as the governing statement:

> We equip athletes with faith-centered prayer support, credentialed spiritual mentorship, and the TAP Time Capsule—a tangible archive of their spiritual journey—delivered directly to their locker rooms.

Supporting expression:

> Excellence on the field. Wholeness off of it.

Long-form explanation:

The Athletes Prayer Foundation exists because an athlete’s spiritual dimension is as critical as their physical or mental attributes. TAP supports the person behind the jersey before, during, and long after the playing years through quiet, consistent prayer, individualized mentorship, and a physical record of spiritual growth.

#### Nonprofit positioning

The site must describe TAP as both:

- A **501(c)(3) nonprofit** organized around athlete spiritual health and lifelong wholeness.
- A **social enterprise** that earns related program revenue through Time Capsule subscriptions, Legacy Gifts, and institutional partnerships.

This is not “a product company with a charitable message,” nor a donation-only ministry. The model is designed so that earned revenue and charitable support reinforce mission durability.

Recommended explanation:

> TAP operates as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit social enterprise. Program fees support the direct cost of personalized Time Capsules, credentialed mentorship, and game-day delivery. Charitable gifts help extend that care to more athletes, schools, and markets.

#### Required verification before publication

The corpus describes TAP as a 501(c)(3), while its legal roadmap also discusses filing and securing tax-exempt documentation. To preserve donor trust, the website must not publish “tax-deductible,” an EIN, or an IRS determination claim until the corresponding documentation is available for display or download.

The trust center must distinguish clearly among:

- Legal status completed.
- Applications pending.
- Grants committed.
- Funds received.
- Projections.
- Actual operating results.

No projection may be presented as impact already achieved.

#### Lean-organization narrative

TAP should present its size as a discipline, not a deficiency:

- Founder-led by Michael Drew.
- Atlanta-based.
- High-touch and relationship-driven.
- Governed through a planned working board of 3–5 members.
- Supported by credentialed contract chaplains and a sports-community Advisory Circle.
- Built around “Character and Access,” with rigorous attention to discretion, logistics, and locker-room conduct.

The site should not manufacture the visual appearance of a large national institution. Credibility comes from specificity: who is responsible, how delivery works, what the program costs, how funds are used, and what has or has not yet been completed.

### 4. The identity-crisis thesis

#### Core thesis

Elite sports systematically train and protect the performer while frequently leaving the person’s spiritual identity underdeveloped.

Athletes are rewarded, evaluated, and publicly defined through performance. When playing time, health, roster status, or an entire career changes, the identity built around the jersey can collapse with it. TAP calls this the **Identity Void** or **Locker Room Void**.

The site should frame the problem as a sequence:

1. **Performance becomes identity.**  
   The athlete’s value is repeatedly tied to statistics, selection, contracts, recruiting, and public approval.

2. **Support is often temporary.**  
   A huddle, speech, app session, or pre-game message may be meaningful, but it frequently leaves no enduring record for the athlete.

3. **Transition removes the system.**  
   Injury, transfer, graduation, being cut, or retirement can abruptly remove the team structure around which the athlete’s identity has been organized.

4. **The athlete lacks a tangible history beyond performance.**  
   Highlight reels preserve what the athlete did. TAP’s archive is intended to preserve who the athlete was becoming.

#### Evidence in the corpus

The site’s evidence model should use three layers.

##### Layer 1: The post-career crisis statistic

The venture corpus states:

> Between 60% and 78% of professional athletes face a financial or personal crisis within five years of retirement.

This is TAP’s strongest urgency statistic, but the supplied corpus does not include a primary citation. Before public launch:

- Attach a named source and publication date.
- Define which leagues or athlete populations the percentage describes.
- Avoid collapsing “financial crisis,” “personal crisis,” depression, and spiritual distress into a single clinical conclusion.
- Use “the corpus cites” or “research indicates” only when the source is visible.

Until verified, the hero should not present “78%” as an unqualified scientific fact. It may appear in a sourced education module or founder perspective once substantiated.

##### Layer 2: The observable support gap

The corpus identifies three concrete gaps:

- **The Spiritual Void:** Material success does not necessarily resolve loneliness, anxiety, or performance-based identity.
- **The Ephemerality of Support:** Generic huddles and pre-game talks can be forgotten once the game begins.
- **The Lack of Tangible Heritage:** Athletes commonly retain statistics, film, awards, and contracts but not a physical archive of spiritual reflection.

This is the most defensible narrative foundation because it explains the mechanism TAP addresses without making a medical claim.

##### Layer 3: TAP’s measurable intervention

TAP does not claim to “solve” every post-career crisis. It provides a structured intervention that can be tracked:

- Personalized Time Capsules delivered.
- Identity-wellness check-ins completed.
- Athletes receiving credentialed mentorship.
- Season-to-archive retention.
- Athlete referral rate.
- Team and school partnerships.
- Locker-room or facility access points.
- Athletes continuing into post-career archival support.

Recommended copy:

> The crisis does not begin at retirement. It begins when performance becomes the only available answer to “Who am I?” TAP builds another answer—one prayer, one conversation, and one physical archive at a time.

#### Solution logic

The TAP Time Capsule turns an intangible act into a repeatable physical ritual:

1. A personalized prayer scroll is prepared.
2. The scroll is delivered to the athlete’s locker, home, or approved training facility.
3. The athlete uses it as a brief grounding touchpoint around game day.
4. Credentialed mentorship supports the reflection behind the artifact.
5. Scrolls accumulate into a career-long archive.
6. The archive remains when a season, team, or career ends.

The product should never be presented as a good-luck charm or guaranteed performance enhancer. Its purpose is spiritual grounding and identity continuity.

### 5. The two revenue engines

## Engine A: Program subscriptions and purchases

### Pro Individual

- **Price:** $85–$120 per game.
- **Audience:** Professional and elite athletes.
- **Includes:** Personalized Time Capsule delivery and weekly one-to-one chaplain access.
- **Primary message:** A private, consistent spiritual ritual built for the pressures and logistics of professional sports.
- **Primary CTA:** `Explore Pro Support`
- **Conversion:** Private enrollment inquiry before payment, because access, schedule, league, and delivery method must be qualified.

### Student-Athlete

- **Price:** $45–$85 per game.
- **Audience:** High school and collegiate athletes.
- **Includes:** A streamlined Time Capsule experience centered on “identity beyond the jersey.”
- **Related program:** A 12-week spiritual curriculum is part of the planned HS/College Edition.
- **Primary message:** Build the foundation before the pressure of the next level.
- **Primary CTA:** `Explore Student Support`

### Legacy Gift

- **Price:** $150–$250.
- **Audience:** Parents, families, alumni, and supporters.
- **Purpose:** Purchase a prayer archive for a specific athlete.
- **Primary message:** Be present spiritually when you cannot be in the locker room physically.
- **Primary CTA:** `Give a Legacy Gift`

### Team and institutional programs

- **Price:** $2,500–$8,000 per season.
- **Audience:** Athletic directors, coaches, schools, private training academies, and team partners.
- **Primary message:** Credentialed spiritual support and tangible prayer archives for a roster.
- **Primary CTA:** `Discuss a Team Season`

Team partnerships belong in the program engine even when a donor, booster, or alumnus underwrites the cost. The site must identify:

- Who receives the service.
- Who pays for it.
- Whether payment is a program fee, sponsorship, restricted donation, or grant.
- What tax treatment applies to the transaction.

## Engine B: Philanthropy

### Major gifts

Major gifts fund mission capacity rather than a consumer benefit. Priorities in the corpus include:

- The $150,000 startup funding and sponsorship goal.
- The first 10 high school partnerships.
- Chaplain credentialing.
- Initial Time Capsule production.
- Atlanta locker-room logistics.
- Regional expansion.
- Athlete access regardless of a school’s budget.
- A three-month cash reserve.
- The planned $2,000-per-month Mission Fund for athletes in financial crisis.

### Recurring giving

Recurring gifts should be framed around continuity:

> Quiet consistency requires dependable support. A recurring gift helps TAP maintain mentorship, production, and delivery through the changing rhythms of the sports calendar.

Suggested giving interface:

- One-time or monthly toggle.
- Amount selection, including $50.
- Optional restriction to TAPHS, chaplain credentialing, Time Capsule production, or general mission.
- Recognition preference: public, private, or anonymous.
- No merchandise or program entitlement attached to the gift.

### Corporate and foundation partnerships

This route should support:

- Grants.
- Team or school sponsorship.
- Athlete wellness partnerships.
- Time Capsule production.
- Chaplain credentialing.
- Atlanta or regional market activation.

Primary CTA:

`Request the Partner Brief`

The page should provide Michael Drew’s direct contact information:

- **Michael Drew, Founder**
- **404-988-6181**
- **jmdbenz@gmail.com**

#### Keeping the engines from colliding

| Program transaction | Philanthropic transaction |
|---|---|
| “Enroll,” “Order,” “Gift,” or “Start a Team Season” | “Give,” “Donate,” or “Fund the Mission” |
| Receives a defined program, product, or service | Advances the charitable mission without an equivalent personal benefit |
| Pricing is visible | Giving amount is donor-selected |
| Program checkout or enrollment form | Givebutter or Donorbox donation flow |
| Program confirmation and service terms | Donation receipt and designation |
| Revenue reported as earned program revenue | Revenue reported as charitable contribution |
| Product imagery leads | Mission, impact, governance, and stewardship lead |

Additional separation rules:

1. Keep `Programs` and `Give` as separate top-level navigation items.
2. Do not label a Legacy Gift purchase as a donation.
3. Do not place tax-deductible language beside a subscription price.
4. If a donor underwrites a Team Season, route the transaction through the giving flow and document the restriction.
5. Use separate confirmation pages and email sequences.
6. Report earned and contributed revenue separately in financial summaries.
7. Never suggest that purchasing a Pro subscription is required to receive spiritual care in a crisis.
8. On program pages, the primary CTA is enrollment. On donor pages, the primary CTA is giving. Do not give both equal visual weight within the same viewport.

### 4.7 Receipting and transaction requirements

#### Charitable contribution receipt

Every completed charitable contribution must generate:

- TAP’s legal organization name
- Statement that TAP is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit
- Donor name
- Transaction date
- Gift amount
- Gift frequency
- Gift designation
- Unique transaction or receipt ID
- Payment method type without full account data
- Statement regarding whether goods or services were provided
- If goods or services were provided, a description and good-faith value supplied by TAP’s authorized finance process
- Contact information for correction requests
- Recurring-gift management instructions for monthly gifts

#### Product and service transactions

Pro Individual subscriptions, Student-Athlete services, Team Season partnerships, and Family/Gift Edition purchases must not automatically receive a charitable-contribution receipt.

They require:

- Purchase or service confirmation
- Amount paid
- Product or service description
- Order or invoice ID
- Billing date
- Fulfillment or follow-up status
- Contact information

If a transaction contains both a charitable contribution and goods or services, create separate line items and use only finance-approved deductible language.

#### DAF requirements

- Record the sponsoring organization as the legal donor when appropriate.
- Do not send a tax receipt to the recommending individual for the DAF grant.
- Send an acknowledgement that does not imply a second deduction.
- Do not provide donor benefits in exchange for a DAF grant.

#### Stock requirements

- Acknowledge the property received and receipt date.
- Do not state the property’s deductible value.
- Maintain transfer records outside the public website.

#### Failed and refunded payments

- Send a failed-payment notice without sensitive account details.
- Retry recurring charges only according to processor rules.
- Send refund confirmation showing the refunded amount and transaction ID.
- Synchronize refunds and chargebacks to the CRM and analytics system.
- Reissue corrected receipts when a designation, donor name, or transaction amount is formally corrected.

### 5.5 Accessibility requirements

Build and test to **WCAG 2.1 Level AA**.

#### Required implementation

- Semantic landmarks: `header`, `nav`, `main`, `aside`, `footer`.
- One descriptive `h1` per page.
- Logical heading hierarchy without skipped levels.
- Keyboard access for every control.
- Visible focus state with at least 3:1 contrast against adjacent colors.
- Body-text contrast of at least 4.5:1.
- Large-text and graphical-control contrast of at least 3:1.
- Do not use gold for small text unless the specific color pairing passes.
- Every form field must have a persistent label.
- Required fields must be identified in text, not by color alone.
- Errors must:
  - Appear inline
  - Be summarized at the top
  - Link to the affected field
  - Be announced through an ARIA live region
- Modal focus must be trapped and restored on close.
- Mobile-navigation focus must be contained while open.
- Images require meaningful alt text or empty alt text when decorative.
- Videos require captions; meaningful audio requires a transcript.
- Autoplay audio is prohibited.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Donation and inquiry flows must work at 200% browser zoom.
- Content must reflow at 320 CSS pixels without two-dimensional scrolling, excluding necessary data tables.
- Use native radio, checkbox, button, and select elements wherever possible.
- Session timeouts, if introduced by a payment provider, must be announced and recoverable.
