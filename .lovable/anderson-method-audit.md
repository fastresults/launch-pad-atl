## Executive Summary

You are sitting on more brand equity than the site is currently pricing in.

Between the eight-stage framework, the 60+ vetted startup ideas, the $297 in-room workshop, the eight follow-on build workshops, and Adam Anderson's own thirty-year operator résumé, StartupLabs already has all the raw material of a signature methodology. What's missing is the **name, the frame, and the doctrine** that turns a workshop into a movement.

This report does three things:

1. **Audits** every section of home page copy and all 60 startup ideas against the four audiences you're actually trying to reach.
2. **Codifies** the underlying operator logic — the thing Adam has been doing intuitively for thirty years and now delivers in one morning — as **The Anderson Method™**, a seven-stage, teachable system with its own vocabulary, principles, and diagram.
3. **Delivers** a concrete rewrite kit and a 30/60/90 rollout so the Method becomes the through-line on every surface: home page, sales page, emails, social, workshops.

The single most important finding: the site's current copy is *tactical* (14 days, $297, first customer). The Anderson Method is *transformational* (from stuck to shipping, from W-2 to owner, from "someday" to "Monday"). Adopting it doesn't replace what's already there — it gives the tactics a story big enough to carry them.

**The one-line reframe this report is arguing for:**

> The 14-Day Launch Method is *what happens in the room.*
> The Anderson Method is *why it works — and why nothing else has to.*

## 2. The Audience the Site Is Actually Talking To

Every strategic finding in this report tests against four archetypes. Get sharper on these four and the rest of the funnel gets easier — headline, imagery, CTA copy, ad targeting, workshop room energy.

### Archetype A — *The Aspiring First-Time Founder*
- **Life state:** has an itch, no playbook. Reads Founders' Podcasts on the commute, has a Notes app graveyard, has told three people "I've been thinking about starting something."
- **Deepest fear:** wasting the next 12 months on the wrong idea, or spending money on a logo before there's a business to logo.
- **What they secretly want from the site:** permission, a smaller first step, and someone who's obviously done it themselves.
- **Where the site already speaks to them:** the *"Stop turning the startup over in your head"* bottom CTA is nearly perfect for this archetype.
- **Where the site currently misses:** hero uses "operator-led method quietly replacing accelerators" — vocabulary this archetype doesn't yet own. They don't know they wanted to *not* attend an accelerator; they just wanted to start something.

### Archetype B — *The Plan-B Seeker*
- **Life state:** W-2 professional, 32–52, has heard the layoff rumor twice this year, has a spouse and a mortgage, has been running spreadsheets on how much runway they'd need.
- **Deepest fear:** quitting the job and being unable to replace the paycheck within 90 days.
- **What they secretly want:** a *staircase* — proof they can start on nights and weekends without blowing up the day job, then step down when the numbers cross.
- **Where the site already speaks to them:** the "Side hustle → full-time" category filter and the 5 "Side" ideas nail this archetype's fantasy.
- **Where the site currently misses:** none of the hero copy names this archetype directly. "Plan B" doesn't appear anywhere. The Method doesn't yet have a *staircase* — the honest ladder from $2k side income to $15k full income.

### Archetype C — *The Main Street / Trades Operator*
- **Life state:** already handy — barber, tradesperson, mobile detailer, groomer, small crew leader. May already have revenue. Wants leverage, systems, and a real brand instead of a Facebook page and a burner number.
- **Deepest fear:** the site, the LLC, the marketing, the "tech stuff" — all the boring scaffolding that keeps them stuck at owner-operator with no time.
- **What they secretly want:** someone to say the tech doesn't have to be their job, and to hand them the parts that are.
- **Where the site already speaks to them:** the Main Street track callout in the hero, "Atlanta-built · Founder-first," and 14 of the 60 ideas being explicitly Main Street.
- **Where the site currently misses:** they don't see themselves in phrases like "AI Prompt Library" or "Website PRD." Those are strategy-consultant words. They need "we set up the LLC, the website, the answering service, and the ads — in one morning."

### Archetype D — *The Family / Couple Operator*
- **Life state:** two adults, often with kids, want to build something together — an Airbnb management team, a cleaning crew, a handyman crew, a Saturday car wash that teaches the kids. Trust is high. Time is low.
- **Deepest fear:** blowing up the household finances or the relationship by taking a bad swing.
- **What they secretly want:** a business that *fits their life* — one that plays to each partner's strengths, doesn't require a co-working membership, and can be run from the kitchen table.
- **Where the site already speaks to them:** the "Family-run" filter and 4 ideas (two-truck handyman, husband-wife Airbnb management, Saturday family car wash, family Airbnb cleaning crew).
- **Where the site currently misses:** the imagery, the testimonials, and the framework language are all *individual-founder-coded*. Nothing shows a couple standing next to a truck, or two partners at the workshop table. This archetype needs to see themselves twice.

**Design implication:** Every hero variant, section, and email CTA in the rewrite kit will name at least one of these four archetypes explicitly. The site currently addresses "founders" — a word that flatters the aspiring first-timer and alienates the other three.

## 3. Current-State Audit of the Home Page

The home page has ten sections, in order:
1. Hero
2. Video Testimonials
3. Framework (8 stages, 60+ deliverables)
4. Business Ideas Scroller (60 ideas, filterable)
5. Honest Roadmap (Foundation-first argument + What $297 gets you + Build Layer of 8 workshops)
6. Facilitator (Adam Anderson bio)
7. Services Teaser
8. Venue
9. Bottom CTA
10. Footer

Each section is scored on the **PPPP** rubric — *Permission, Proof, Path, Payoff* — against the four archetypes. 1 = missing, 5 = excellent.

### 3.1 Hero

**Current headline:** *"The 14-Day Launch Method. First paying customer in two weeks."*
**Current subhead (paraphrased):** *"The operator-led method quietly replacing accelerators, courses, and raw AI. Run live by Adam. $297 once, yours forever."*

| PPPP | A: First-timer | B: Plan-B | C: Main St | D: Family |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Permission** — do they see themselves? | 4 | 2 | 3 | 2 |
| **Proof** — do they believe it? | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| **Path** — do they know the next step? | 4 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| **Payoff** — do they *feel* the life change? | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 |

**Findings:**
- The Old-Way / New-Way epiphany block is the single strongest piece of copy on the page — keep it, promote it.
- Hero speaks past Archetypes B, C, and D by naming what it's *replacing* (accelerators, courses, raw AI) instead of naming *who it's for*.
- "14-Day Launch Method" is a *tactic name*, not a *transformation name*. It answers *how long*, not *what changes about your life*.
- The ATL Founder-Friendly seal is beautiful and mostly invisible at 30% opacity. That's brand equity you're hiding.

**Anderson Method reframe:** the hero should introduce the *Method* by name, position the 14-day sprint as its flagship application, and name at least two of the four archetypes in the sub-header.

### 3.2 Video Testimonials

Currently a strong section for social proof but not audited here for content (video content is out of scope). **Recommendation:** add captions/pull-quotes that map each testimonial to the archetype it best represents — "Plan-B seeker who left corporate marketing," "husband-wife team who now run 22 Airbnbs" — so viewers self-select faster.

### 3.3 Framework (8 stages, 60+ deliverables)

**Current framing:** *"Inside The 14-Day Launch Method — A business ready to take money. Built with Adam in one morning."*

This is the site's strongest section by information density and the section most in danger of overwhelming Archetypes B, C, and D.

| PPPP | A | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Permission | 4 | 3 | 2 | 2 |
| Proof | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 |
| Path | 4 | 3 | 2 | 2 |
| Payoff | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 |

**Findings:**
- 60+ deliverables is *proof-of-thoroughness gold* but a *choice-overload risk*. First-timers scan. Plan-B seekers scroll. Main St operators bounce.

**Anderson Method reframe:** overlay the seven Anderson stages on top of the eight framework stages as a *lens*. Same deliverables, new framing. The framework becomes "how we deliver the Method," not the Method itself.

Newly upgraded with the AI-insight hover popover (ideal operator, why it's smart, annual potential). This is now the site's most differentiated piece — no competitor has this.

**Findings:**
- Category filters ("Online / Main Street / Service / Food & Hands / Side hustle → Full-time / Family-run") are the *only place on the site where all four archetypes see themselves labeled*. This is the archetype system in disguise. Promote it.

- Missing: any CTA on the ideas. A visitor who falls in love with "Pop-up axe-throwing nights" has *no next step*. They should be able to click into a dedicated concept page or add the idea to a starter kit that follows them into signup.

### 3.5 Honest Roadmap

Three-act structure: *Why foundation first → What $297 gets you → What comes after your first customer (8 workshops).*

**Findings:**
- "Every founder we've watched fail did the same thing…" is *the single most authoritative sentence on the site*. Move it higher.
- The Foundation-First trio of cards is the most defensible IP after the ideas library — it's the doctrine.
- The 8-workshop Build Layer is a real product ladder but visually reads like a card grid. Turning it into a *chapter arc* ("after you've launched, here's the next 8 half-days that turn revenue into a real business") converts it from a menu into a promise.

### 3.6 Facilitator

Adam Anderson — 30 years of operating, Citigroup / Mayo / 3M / Disney credentials, digital systems for a Caribbean government, five years running a regional summit.

| PPPP | A | B | C | D |

- **Services Teaser** — too short to carry weight. Should preview 2–3 flagship services with pricing so visitors don't have to click blind.

- **Bottom CTA** — *"Stop turning the startup over in your head"* is the second-best headline on the site. Consider A/B testing it as the hero.
- **Footer** — out of scope for this audit; verify the archetype filter links point to `/#business-ideas?filter=side` etc. so archetype-specific ad campaigns can deep-link.

## 6. Introducing The Anderson Method™

### 6.1 Why the Method needs a name

Right now, StartupLabs has three named things: **the workshop** ($297), **the sprint** (14-Day Launch Method), and **the facilitator** (Adam Anderson). What it does not yet have is a name for the *underlying philosophy* that makes all three work.

Every category-defining brand has one:
- David Allen has **Getting Things Done**.
- Eric Ries has **The Lean Startup**.
- Alex Hormozi has **$100M Offers**.
- Marie Kondo has **The KonMari Method**.

In every case, the named method is the *IP*, and the products (books, workshops, coaching) are its *distribution vehicles*. The name gives the founder ownership of the category, defensible pricing, and a vocabulary customers can teach to each other.

**The Anderson Method™** is that name for StartupLabs. It positions Adam as the author of a doctrine — not merely the host of a workshop.

### 6.2 Origin story (draft, 250 words — for the About page)

*Adam Anderson spent thirty years starting companies. He built and sold agencies. He shipped digital systems for Citigroup, Mayo Clinic, 3M, and Disney. He built the technology backbone for a Caribbean nation's government. He produced one of the region's largest business summits, five years running. And — the part that matters most — he sat in the founder's seat again and again, in tech, in services, on Main Street.*

*What he noticed was this: every founder who failed did the same thing. They spent six months and $30,000 building a logo, a website, and ads that told nobody a coherent story — and never once asked a real customer for money. Every founder who succeeded did the opposite. They named an offer, priced it, went to a real human being, and asked. Everything else — the polish, the systems, the scale — came after.*

*That pattern became a doctrine. The doctrine became a repeatable seven-stage method. The method became a workshop that ships you from "good idea" to "first paying customer" in fourteen days. Adam calls it, without much ceremony, the Anderson Method.*

### 6.3 The seven stages

The Anderson Method is built on seven verbs. Each is chosen because it names an *action a founder can take today* — not a status, not a checkpoint, not a deliverable.

The mnemonic is **ANDERSON** minus the vowels — **ND-R-S-N** — reframed as *seven acts* the founder walks through in order. (Full naming: "The Anderson Seven.")

| # | Verb | The Principle | The Founder's Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Anchor** | Pick the archetype you actually are. Refuse the founder cosplay. | "Which of the four founders am I today — and can I say it out loud?" |
| 2 | **Name** | Commit to a concrete idea from the library. Kill the second idea in the drawer. | "Can I say my business in one sentence — starting with a verb?" |
| 3 | **Design** | Shape the offer, the price, and the named first customer. No hypotheticals. | "If I sent this offer to one real person tonight, would they know how to say yes?" |
| 4 | **Equip** | Assemble the minimum toolkit — LLC, permits, brand, booking, AI stack. | "If a customer paid today, could I take their money and deliver?" |
| 5 | **Reach** | Open the one channel. Send the first fifty asks. | "Have I asked fifty real humans for money this week?" |
| 6 | **Ship** | Take the first dollar in ≤14 days. Celebrate. Then take the second. | "Have I invoiced?" |
| 7 | **Scale** | Climb the honest staircase from side income to full life-change. | "Do I know what has to be true for me to hire the next person?" |

Each stage is a *doorway*, not a *deliverable*. The 60+ framework deliverables map into the stages as the *how* — not the *what*.

### 6.4 The three doctrines (the "Foundation First" trilogy, renamed)

The Anderson Method has three non-negotiable doctrines. Each answers a mistake Adam has watched founders make thousands of times.

**Doctrine 1 — Foundation before flair.**
> A wrong-headed brand is expensive to undo. Anchor and Name before you commission a logo. If you Equip before you Design, you'll ship polish for a business that doesn't exist yet.

**Doctrine 2 — Buyer before builder.**
> A great website with no ICP doesn't convert. Reach before you build. If you don't know the fifty humans you're selling to by name, you don't have a marketing problem — you have a *business* problem.

**Doctrine 3 — Sharpness before scale.**
> AI amplifies whatever it's pointed at. Ship before you Scale. If the offer is fuzzy, AI will scale the fuzz. If the offer is sharp, AI will scale you.

These three doctrines already exist on the site as the "Why foundation first" trio. Renaming and elevating them turns three loose insights into a *creed* customers can quote back.

### 6.6 Why this Method is *actually* new (defensible against competitors)

Three things make the Anderson Method genuinely differentiated, not just re-labelled Lean Startup:

1. **Archetype-first, not idea-first.** Lean Startup, Y-Combinator, most accelerators assume you already know your archetype. Anderson starts by naming it — because a Plan-B seeker and a Main Street operator need *different first moves*.
2. **Library-anchored, not blank-page.** The Method is delivered against a 60-idea library, not a founder's blank canvas. This is what compresses the sprint from 12 weeks to 14 days.
3. **Operator-led, not curriculum-led.** The Method is delivered by Adam in the room. That's not a delivery preference — it's a design constraint. The Method assumes the founder gets *live judgment* from someone who's shipped, on the *specific* offer they're pricing.

Any accelerator can claim two of these. None currently claim all three. That's the moat.
