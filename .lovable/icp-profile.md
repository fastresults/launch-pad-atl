# Startuplabs — ICP Profile

*Internal reference. Source of truth for copy, ads, sales calls, and disqualification.*
*Voice: plain-spoken, 6th–8th grade, warm, no jargon. Same voice as the live site.*

---

## 1. The One-Line ICP

**A 30–45-year-old W-2 employee in metro Atlanta (or joining on Zoom from anywhere in the US) who has an idea for a startup they'd own, no clear plan to start it, and is willing to spend one focused morning and $297 to stop guessing and walk out with a real plan.**

Everything below is a variation on that person.

---

## 2. Who's actually in the room (the whole audience)

| Attribute | Range |
|---|---|
| Age | 28–55, concentrated 32–48 |
| Household income | $65k–$180k (dual income common) |
| Employment | 70% currently W-2, 20% recently laid off / between jobs, 10% already running something small |
| Geography | Atlanta metro in-person; US-wide on Zoom (heavy Southeast) |
| Education | Some college through master's; roughly half have a 4-year degree |
| Family stage | Married with kids at home is the median; single professionals and empty-nesters also present |
| Tech comfort | Uses their phone for everything. Can figure out Zoom, Canva, Shopify with help. Not a coder. |
| Prior startup experience | None or one small side attempt that fizzled |
| Money on the line | Can lose $297 without a fight at home. Can't lose $5,000. |

**What they all share:** they've been "about to start" for 6+ months. They have tabs open. They have a Notion doc. They don't have a customer.

## 3. The four primary personas

### A. The Plan-B Seeker *(most common buyer — ~45% of registrations)*

**Snapshot.** 34, nurse or teacher or ops manager at a mid-size company. Household income $110k combined. Two kids under 10. Lives in Marietta, Kennesaw, Decatur, Duluth, or on Zoom from Charlotte / Nashville / Jacksonville. Salary is fine. The ceiling on that salary is not fine.

**What they say out loud.**
> "I'm not trying to quit tomorrow. I just want something that's mine."
> "If I could bring in an extra two grand a month, that changes everything."
> "I've been thinking about this for a year and I haven't done anything."

**What keeps them up at 11pm.**
1. Watching a coworker they don't respect get promoted over them.
2. Doing the math on what daycare + one kid activity + one car repair does to their paycheck.
3. The feeling that if they don't start something in the next 12 months, they never will.

**What they've tried.** A YouTube rabbit hole. Half a Skool community. One coach's free webinar that turned into a $2,000 pitch. A Notion template they paid $19 for. None of it produced a customer.

**Success at 6 months.** One paying customer. A business name they're proud to say at a cookout. $500–$2,000/mo coming in on the side. A plan for how to grow it without quitting the day job yet.

**Trigger to buy.** A specific bad Monday. A spouse asking "so when are you actually going to do this?" A friend announcing their business is working. A birthday ending in 0 or 5.

**Enters through.** In-person Foundation workshop in Atlanta ($297) or Zoom Foundation. Most likely to bring a spouse or friend.

**Objections + answers.**
- *"$297 is a lot for one morning."* — It's one dinner out. The next 90 days of guessing costs $13,900.
- *"I don't have an idea yet."* — Bring the direction. We'll help you name the startup before you leave.
- *"What if I don't finish?"* — You finish. That's what the room is for.

---

### B. The AI-Displaced Professional *(fastest-growing segment — ~25%)*

**Snapshot.** 42, was a marketing manager / paralegal / analyst / mid-level designer / customer success lead. Laid off in the last 6 months, or watching their team shrink quarter over quarter. Household income was $140k, now $70k on unemployment + spouse. Has $20k–$60k in savings. Has been applying for jobs for 4 months and hearing crickets or "we went with someone more senior/junior/cheaper."

**What they say out loud.**
> "I've applied to 200 jobs. I've had 6 interviews. The market isn't coming back for people like me."
> "I don't want to build a résumé anymore. I want to build a thing."
> "I've got maybe 8 months of runway. I need to move."

**What keeps them up at 11pm.**
1. The gap on their résumé getting longer.
2. Watching their savings tick down every month with nothing coming back in.
3. The quiet fear that the career they trained for is gone and nobody's told them what replaces it.

**What they've tried.** LinkedIn premium. A career coach who told them to "lean into their brand." One consulting client from an old coworker that paid $2,500 and never came back. A cursory look at franchises.

**Success at 6 months.** A business making $3k–$8k/mo — enough to breathe. A clear pipeline of the next 3 customers. Confidence that they're not going back to job applications.

**Trigger to buy.** The unemployment check running out. A rejection from a job they thought they'd get. Watching a peer announce their own consultancy on LinkedIn.

**Enters through.** Zoom Foundation (they're often not in Atlanta), often followed within 30 days by a done-with-you engagement because they have both urgency *and* some savings to deploy.

**Objections + answers.**
- *"I should keep job hunting instead."* — Do both. One focused morning won't cost you a job you're going to get.
- *"I'm too senior to start from zero."* — Your last 15 years are the reason your first customer says yes on day one.
- *"I need income now, not in six months."* — Foundation gets your first paid customer in two weeks, not six months.

---

### C. The Main Street Operator *(~20%)*

**Snapshot.** 38, already runs something — a two-truck lawn crew, a hair studio in a spare room, a food truck, a small bookkeeping practice, a real-estate side hustle, a mobile detailing van. Making $40k–$120k gross but it feels chaotic. Books are in three places. Pricing was set two years ago and never revisited. No website they're proud of. All growth is word-of-mouth and they're maxed out on that.

**What they say out loud.**
> "I'm working 60 hours and I still don't know if I'm making money."
> "I know I could charge more. I don't know how much."
> "I need this to feel like a real business, not a hustle."

**What keeps them up at 11pm.**
1. A big customer leaving without warning.
2. Whether it's time to hire, and whether they can afford to.
3. The feeling that they've hit a ceiling and don't know why.

**What they've tried.** A logo from Fiverr. A QuickBooks trial they didn't finish setting up. One Facebook ad that lost $400. A conversation with a "business coach" that felt like a sales pitch.

**Success at 6 months.** Clear pricing that reflects what they're actually worth. Books that tell them the truth. One repeatable way to get new customers that isn't "hope somebody refers me." A shot at their first hire.

**Trigger to buy.** A tax bill that hurts. Losing a customer they thought was locked in. A spouse asking to see the numbers. A price hike from a supplier they can't pass on.

**Enters through.** In-person Foundation in Atlanta (they're local). Common upsell: done-with-you for the pricing + brand cleanup they've been putting off.

**Objections + answers.**
- *"I already run a business — this is for beginners."* — Half the room is people like you. Foundation is where you fix what you skipped when you started.
- *"I don't have a free morning."* — You have mornings. You're just spending them working *in* the startup. This one you spend *on* it.
- *"I don't need a website / brand / plan."* — You need pricing you can defend and a way to get the next 10 customers. That's what you'll leave with.

---

### D. The Family / Couple Operator *(~10%)*

**Snapshot.** A married couple, or a parent + adult child, or two close friends going in together. One is usually the driver ("we should do this"), the other is the skeptic who came along ("I'll believe it when I see it"). Combined income is stable but they want something they own together. Often looking at Airbnb management, a cleaning crew, a food concept, a mobile service, or a shop.

**What they say out loud.**
> "We've been talking about doing this together for years."
> "We need to be on the same page before we spend a dollar."
> "I want something we can hand our kids one day."

**What keeps them up at 11pm.**
1. Fighting about money if the business struggles.
2. One person doing all the work while the other "helps."
3. Making the leap and finding out they had different versions of the plan in their heads.

**What they've tried.** Long conversations at the kitchen table that end in "let's talk about it later." One shared Google Doc that neither of them opened again. A conversation with a friend who runs a similar startup that was helpful but too casual to act on.

**Success at 6 months.** One shared plan they both signed off on. Clear roles ("I handle sales, you handle ops"). First customer. First fight *avoided* because the plan already answered the question.

**Trigger to buy.** An anniversary. A tax return that's bigger than expected. The kid going to school full-time. A joint decision that "this year is the year."

**Enters through.** In-person Foundation — they want to be in the room together. Two seats, $594. Often the highest satisfaction cohort because they leave aligned.

**Objections + answers.**
- *"Do we both need to come?"* — Yes. This is the alignment you've been trying to force at the kitchen table.
- *"$594 for both of us is a lot."* — It's the cheapest marriage counseling money can buy, and you get a business at the end.
- *"What if we disagree in the room?"* — Good. Better to disagree here than after you've spent $10k on the wrong direction.

## 4. Buying triggers (cross-persona)

These are the life events that flip someone from "someday" to "I registered":

1. **A layoff** — theirs or a close coworker's.
2. **A birthday ending in 0 or 5** — the "if not now, when" moment.
3. **A spouse income change** — bonus that funds the leap, or a cut that forces it.
4. **A specific dollar goal getting harder** — private school, a house, a debt payoff.
5. **A friend's business win** — "if they can do it..."
6. **A tax bill or bad Monday** — the acute pain that makes the abstract pain real.
7. **A second baby, or a kid going to school full-time** — the schedule opens up or the pressure to earn more spikes.

**Copy implication:** most of our best headlines are written to one of these seven moments. If a headline isn't speaking to one of them, it's speaking to nobody.

## 5. Where they are and how they talk

**Where to reach them.**
- Facebook groups (local: Atlanta moms, Marietta parents, career-change groups)
- Instagram Reels (short, faces, one clear promise)
- LinkedIn (Persona B especially — the AI-Displaced)
- Nextdoor (Personas C and D)
- Local church + community bulletin (Personas A, C, D)
- Podcasts they already listen to (Ramsey-adjacent, side-hustle, career-change)
- Referrals from prior attendees (highest-converting channel by far)

**Words they use for their own problem.**
- "side income," "second income," "my own thing," "something that's mine"
- "start a business," "start a shop," "start a store"
- "get out of the rat race," "get off the treadmill"
- "figure it out," "make a plan," "stop guessing"
- "for my family," "for the kids," "for retirement"

**Words they do NOT use** (and we should avoid in outbound copy):
- MVP, GTM, TAM, SAM, CAC, LTV, ICP
- Cohort, funnel, method, framework
- Founder-market fit, product-market fit
- Ideate, iterate, pivot (as a noun)
- Bootstrap, moat, wedge

## 6. Disqualifiers — who this is NOT for

Be honest and fast. Sending the wrong person to a morning session costs everyone.

- **Series A / venture-track founders** looking for an investor deck or a pitch coach. Wrong room.
- **Credential collectors** who buy every course and finish none. We can't fix that in one morning.
- **"Tell me my idea is great" shoppers** who want validation, not pressure-testing.
- **Passive-income believers** who think a business runs itself. It doesn't. We won't pretend it does.
- **Anyone who won't do the work in the room.** The room *is* the offer. If they won't show up present, refund them and move on.
- **Under-18 or full-time student with no income** — the price + time cost isn't the right bet for them yet.

## 7. Offer-to-persona matrix

| Persona | Best entry offer | Price | Likely upsell path | Price ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Plan-B Seeker | Foundation (in-person or Zoom) | $297 | +1 add-on workshop (Social $197 or Legal $197) | ~$800 total |
| B. AI-Displaced | Foundation (Zoom) | $297 | Done-with-you 30–60 day engagement | $3k–$8k |
| C. Main Street | Foundation (in-person) | $297 | Higher-tier workshop ($397) + done-with-you | $1.5k–$5k |
| D. Family / Couple | Foundation ×2 (in-person) | $594 | Done-with-you if they want faster launch | $2k–$6k |

**Rule:** never sell above the ceiling in the first morning session. Foundation earns the right to sell the next thing. Trying to close a $5k engagement in the room breaks trust in the room.

## 8. How to use this doc

**When writing copy.** Pick one persona and one trigger from Section 4. Write the headline to that exact human on that exact day. If you can't name the person, the headline is too generic.

**When running ads.** Persona A → Facebook + Instagram, targeting parents 30–45 in metro Atlanta. Persona B → LinkedIn, targeting "open to work" + industries with recent layoffs. Persona C → Nextdoor + local. Persona D → same as A, but creative shows two people, not one.

**On sales calls / DMs.** First question: *"What's going on that made you look at this today?"* Their answer tells you the persona and the trigger. Match the response.

**Routing on the site.**
- Idea, no business yet → `/build` (Foundation)
- Already running something small → `/build` (Foundation) or a higher-tier workshop
- Wants us to do it for them → `/services`
- Wants 1:1 time with Adam → `/one-on-one`

**Copy check (fast).** Before publishing any page or ad, read it out loud and ask:
1. Would the Plan-B Seeker understand every word?
2. Does it name a real feeling from Section 3?
3. Does it use any word from the "do NOT use" list?
4. Does it end with one clear next step, not three?

If any answer is no, rewrite before publishing.
