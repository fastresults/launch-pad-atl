## 1. What is "Brand" and its subcomponents?

**Brand is Stage 06 of the Startup Labs framework — an identity worth premium pricing. A system, not stickers.**

It is a mentored working session. You bring your Foundation, three brands you admire, and an instinct about voice. Staff apply a brand-lead lens, pressure-test purpose / promise / positioning, and direct the visual work until the identity feels intentional instead of accidental. The stage one-liner: **a brand system, not stickers.**

The six assets inside Brand (verbatim from the site):

| # | Asset | What it changes for you |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **What your brand stands for** | The strategic foundation under your brand — purpose, promise, audience, positioning. Every logo, color, and word downstream has a reason behind it, and you stop redoing your brand every time something feels off. |
| 02 | **The words your brand uses** | Your headline, supporting messages, and proof — organized so every page, post, and pitch pulls from the same well. You write faster, sound consistent everywhere, and stop staring at a blinking cursor. |
| 03 | **The look you hand to a designer** | A clear brief for logo, colors, type, and visual feel — ready for a designer or an AI tool. You skip months of revisions and look credible from day one. |
| 04 | **How your brand sounds** | Word choice, rhythm, what to avoid — so anyone writing for you sounds like you. Founders, contractors, and AI tools all produce on-brand copy without you rewriting every sentence. |
| 05 | **Your brand rules, all in one place** | Logo rules, colors, type, voice, and examples in one asset. Hand it to any vendor, freelancer, or new hire and they ship on-brand work without forty rounds of feedback. |
| 06 | **Your logo, favicon, and images** | AI-generation prompts for logo, favicon, OG image, avatar, and email banner — with exact sizes and formats. You walk into Stage 07 ready to ship a site that looks like a real brand. |

## 2. Design system

Use the site's own tokens — do not invent a palette.

**Typefaces**
- Display / headlines: **Outfit** (300–700), tight tracking, semibold for titles.
- Body / UI: **DM Sans** (300–700).

**Color (dark theme is the default surface)**

| Token | Dark | Light |
|---|---|---|
| Background | `oklch(0.14 0.02 280)` — near-black violet | `oklch(0.99 0 0)` |
| Foreground (ink) | `oklch(0.98 0 0)` | `oklch(0.18 0.02 280)` |
| Primary / accent | `oklch(0.68 0.28 290)` — electric violet | `oklch(0.58 0.28 290)` |
| Muted surface | `oklch(0.28 0.04 290)` | `oklch(0.94 0.02 290)` |

**Shape & motion**
- Base radius `0.75rem`; cards step up to `1.25–1.5rem`. No hard corners.
- Each framework stage already carries a hand-drawn animated line mark (single-weight strokes, self-drawing paths, small looping accents in the primary violet, `currentColor` for ink). Match that: line-drawn, restrained, no gradients-on-white, no generic 3D.
- Motion vocabulary: stroke-draw in, gentle 4–6s loops, easing `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` by holding the resting frame.

**Brand motif:** a line-drawn swatch fan that opens like a hand of cards, one blade tipping violet as the direction locks. A single elegant emblem draws itself above it, then a guidelines book closes around the whole set. Restraint is the point — one mark, one accent, nothing decorative.

## 5. Who is it for?

**Prospects visiting the site.** Specifically:
- First-time founders using a free logo maker and a default font.
- Plan-B seekers who need to look credible before they quit anything.
- Main Street and trades operators competing on price because nothing else separates them.
- Family and couple operators who have argued about the logo three times already.

They are not designers. They want to look like a real company and charge accordingly — not to study brand theory.

---

## 6. Tone of copy

**Punchy & bold.** Short declaratives. Second person. No hype adjectives, no design jargon.

House rules that must hold:
- Say **startup**, never "business."
- Say **asset**, never "document."
- Say **framework**, never "template."
- Never frame the offer as a plan, blueprint, playbook, roadmap, or deliverables. It is a **done-with-you build** — name the real artifact (logo concepts generated, palette locked, guidelines book released).

# Attendee brief — your Brand session

*For founders registered for a Startup Labs Brand workshop.*

### What Brand is

Stage 06 — the identity that earns a premium and stops you rebuilding your look every six months. It's a **mentored working session**, 25 minutes of core build time. You bring the raw material; staff apply a brand-lead lens, pressure-test purpose / promise / positioning, and direct the visual work live. Brand is the multiplier on everything Foundation through Governance built.

### Before you arrive

Bring:
- Your Foundation work — one-liner, value prop, and personas.
- Three brands you admire (any category) and one sentence each on *why*.
- Three brands in your category you do **not** want to look like.
- Your instinct about voice: are you the calm expert, the friendly neighbor, or the sharp challenger?
- Any existing marks, colors, or names already in the wild — including the ones you regret.

No design homework. Showing up with a free-logo-maker file is the normal starting condition.

### In the room — the 25-minute core block

**Signature build — strategy, messaging, visual direction**

1. **Brand Strategy Framework** — Purpose · Promise · Audience · Positioning drafted live on a single canvas. Every visual decision downstream has to trace back to one of these four.
2. **Messaging House** — headline plus three supporting messages and three proof points, assembled from your Value Prop and Personas.
3. **Visual direction** — three AI-generated moodboards; you pick one, staff kill the other two. Direction locked, no committee.
4. **Identity generated live** — three logo concepts, a color palette, and a type pairing produced in the room against the locked direction.

Vague answers get sent back. "We want it to feel modern" is not a direction.

### The six assets you walk out with

1. **What your brand stands for** — the Strategy Framework: purpose, promise, audience, positioning.
2. **The words your brand uses** — the Messaging House: headline, supporting messages, proof.
3. **The look you hand to a designer** — the Visual Identity Brief, ready for a designer or an AI tool.
4. **How your brand sounds** — the Voice & Tone one-pager: word choice, rhythm, what to avoid.
5. **Your brand rules, all in one place** — the Brand Guidelines Book, finished on your dashboard.
6. **Your logo, favicon, and images** — three logo concepts (PNG + SVG), palette, type pairing, and exact-size prompts for favicon, OG image, avatar, and email banner.

Staff run the Brand Starter Kit generator after the block: Strategy Framework PDF, Messaging House PDF, Visual Identity Brief DOCX, logo concepts in PNG and SVG, palette and type pairing, and the Voice & Tone one-pager — all staff-reviewed before release to your dashboard.

### Where the work lives

Every asset lands in your dashboard under the Brand stage. It's yours, editable forever. The Brand Studio on your dashboard keeps iterating: adjust palette colors, regenerate marks, and lock the kit when it's right — the same tokens then feed your Website PRD in Stage 07.

### Your first 48 hours after

- Pick your favorite logo concept and run one iteration round on your dashboard.
- Drop the palette and fonts into your existing site and social profiles.
- Finish the Brand Guidelines Book on your dashboard this week.
- Rewrite one page of existing copy using the Messaging House — feel how much faster it goes.

### How you know it worked

A freelancer you've never met can produce on-brand work from your guidelines without you in the room. Your site, your socials, and your pitch sound like the same company. And you can name the reason behind every color, word, and mark — in one sentence each. That's the bar.
